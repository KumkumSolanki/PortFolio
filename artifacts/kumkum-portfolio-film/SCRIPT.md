# Kumkum Solanki — Portfolio Film

## Director's treatment

- **Promise:** Kumkum turns AI/ML and data-science ideas into practical projects.
- **Audience:** Recruiters and engineering teams discovering her portfolio in a vertical social-video feed.
- **Format and runtime:** 9:16 vertical, 26 seconds, looping.
- **Identity:** The existing portfolio's cream, navy, vivid pink, yellow, mint, and lavender; Space Grotesk display type with DM Sans and JetBrains Mono support text.
- **Hero:** Kumkum's supplied portrait, followed by purpose-built motion graphics representing her real projects.
- **Motion:** Snapped kinetic type; aperture and document-frame transformations; a traced data line that changes meaning between projects.
- **Arc:** Abstract idea → named person → featured AI resume project → three rapid applied-AI project reveals → resolved portfolio identity.
- **Payoff:** The project's moving frames resolve into a compact name-and-specialty lockup before the opening ring expands back into the first frame.
- **Audio:** One generated instrumental electronic/ambient bed, no vocals. Aim for a clear lift near 14 seconds and a resolved final phrase; do not claim beat synchronization until the generated track is checked.

## Pre-code review

- The five shots vary between a typographic aperture, a photo-led portrait, a close document interface, three sequential project motifs, and a centered identity end card.
- The subject remains the focus: the opener names practical work, Shot 02 shows Kumkum, Shot 03 explains ResuMatch, Shot 04 names three real projects, and Shot 05 resolves the portfolio identity.
- Every shot changes visibly at least once per second. The final 15–20% of each shot is active handoff motion, not a static dead tail.
- The project visuals are explicitly conceptual diagrams; no fake product screenshots, measurements, model scores, or results are presented.
- The ring, document edge, comparison line, plot line, detection box, and end-card frame provide continuous, subject-led carriers between scenes.
- Text stays within the vertical safe area; portrait and project heroes use at least 70% of the frame's short edge when featured.
- The 26-second timeline has five contiguous 4s, 5s, 5s, 7s, and 5s scenes; no gaps or overlaps in the scene clock.
